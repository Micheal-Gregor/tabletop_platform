| Tile, Slider, Dial, Timer | Admitted kinds | None | None. The Timer's binding is still deferred (ODG-e1) |

So the engine knows what each piece **is** in the game's state, and nothing yet says how a piece **behaves**. A physics
layer adds, per component, the factors (geometry, mass, friction, the player's input), the maths (equations of motion,
in closed form where one exists), the proofs (base cases, invariants, fairness) and the dataset (fitted parameters,
each with its source). The die and the spinner get it now, as deciders. Cards, tokens, pieces and boards are not
touched by the ruling: their outcomes come from rules or geometry, and their physics stays theater.

### The engine: solved already, so reused

- **Rapier's WebAssembly build** is documented as "fully cross-platform deterministic" (matching snapshot hashes on
  every machine), on one condition: starting values come from deterministic operations, not `Math.sin` or `Math.cos`.
  The bench already pins 0.19.3, and open-source dice rollers reportedly use it this way for multiplayer.
- **What the extension's Phase 1 must prove, not assume:** the version is pinned and recorded with the ruleset (an old
  game replays on its own version); throws and flicks are logged as integers; starting values avoid transcendental
  functions; the time step is fixed; each roll uses a fresh world; the settled world's snapshot hash is the replay check.
- **Where it lives.** Rapier moves from bench-only (HK-6 forbids the library from importing it today) into a
  library-tier package. The solver is a decider, so it is injected into the Randomizer binding, as Phase 3's binding
  check requires. Rapier loads asynchronously and the engine's submit is synchronous; Phase 1 decides the load rule.
- **What retires:** the offset that steered the die onto the seeded face, and the 3 g gravity chosen for a "snappy"
  feel. A simulation uses 1 g; snappiness becomes the view's playback speed, an honest presentation choice.

### The die

- **Factors.** Edge length, mass and corner rounding; restitution and friction against felt and rails; the tray's
  walls; the throw (position, velocity and spin from the gesture, rounded to integers in the intent).
- **Proofs, base cases first.** No throw, a die settled cocked against a wall, or one not settled within a stated
  number of steps is refused with "re-roll" (a refusal, never a repair).
- **Fairness.** A dyno of 10,000 simulated throws per preset, chi-square per face; corner rounding, the known bias
  source, is a stated parameter.
- **Dataset.** Published restitution and friction for acrylic dice on felt, checked by filmed drop tests. The bench's
  tuned numbers (felt friction 0.7, launch 2.0 to 3.4 m/s) enter as "assumed" until a measurement replaces them.

### The spinner

- **Factors.** Sectors, whose widths are data so that weighted wheels work. The body, an arrow or a wheel (moment of
  inertia I, radius r). Bearing friction torque τ (constant). Air drag b·ω. Pegs and a flapper, losing energy ΔE at
  each peg. The flick, ω0 = tangential speed ÷ grab radius, logged as an integer. The start angle.
- **Motion between pegs:** I·dω/dt = −τ − b·ω. Time to stop T = (I/b)·ln(1 + bω0/τ); angle turned
  Θ = (I/b)·(ω0 − (τ/b)·ln(1 + bω0/τ)); with no drag, Θ = Iω0²/2τ. Each peg takes away ΔE.
- **The spinner proves the engine.** It runs in Rapier too (a hinge with friction, pegs as colliders), and its closed
  form becomes a vector: Rapier's spin against the formula, within a stated tolerance, before the die relies on Rapier.
- **Proofs, base cases first.** Less than one turn is refused with "re-spin". τ = 0 is refused at admission (a spinner
  must stop). Θ rises strictly with ω0. With ΔE > 0 the arrow never rests on a line. The energy books balance,
  ½Iω0² = friction work + peg losses: an invariant checked like the ledger's.
- **Fairness.** Poincaré's roulette argument (the method of arbitrary functions): once flicks spread Θ over several
  turns, the landing sector tends to uniform. Measured: a dyno of 10,000 flicks per preset, chi-square against the
  sector widths, and a skill index, the ω0 precision one sector needs, (2π/N)·τ/(Iω0), against real flicks' spread.
- **Dataset.** 20 to 30 flicks of one or two real spinners, filmed at 240 fps. Fit τ/I, b/I and ΔE/I, and hold back a
  third to test the predictions. Each preset's numbers say where they came from: measured, published or assumed.

### Phases 2 and 3 (draft), and the tie to mechanics

**Classes:** a shared PhysicsWorld (the pinned engine), Throw and Flick (integer inputs), Jitter, Settle (the outcome
and its snapshot hash), Trajectory (what the view draws), Dyno and Preset; then DieBody and DieSolver, SpinnerGeometry,
SpinnerBody and SpinSolver; and the refusals. **Facets:** the engine seam, Die, Spinner, Data and fitting, Dyno, and
the Read surface Phase 5 binds to. **Vectors,** computed: the spinner's formula against Rapier, the energy books, each
preset's dyno, the fits' residuals, and a replay of logged throws to identical snapshot hashes.

**In the game:** intents `die:throw {die, throw}` and `spinner:spin {spinner, ω0}`. The seed supplies only a jitter u
(input in effect ω0·(1 + ε·u)): replay stays exact, and with ε > 0 no player can aim (ε is yours, per component). The
Spinner kind is superseded to carry its resting angle as well as its sector.
